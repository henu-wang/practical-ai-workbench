## Copy a professional follow-up email template

```text
Subject: [Existing subject, or Follow-up: specific topic]

Hi [Recipient name],

I'm following up on [specific message or request] from [verified date].
Could you [one clear action or question]?

Thank you,
[Your name]
```

Reply in the existing thread when it gives the recipient useful context, keeping its subject. For a new message, use a specific subject such as “Follow-up: orientation room layout”. Remove every placeholder. If the earlier date is uncertain, identify the message by a verified topic instead of guessing.

Need a reply by a real planning date? Replace the question with “If possible, could you [action] by [date] so I can [actual planning need]?” Treat that as a new request. Write “as agreed” or “the deadline you gave me” only when the earlier text supports it. Omit a deadline when it adds nothing.

## Fill six inputs before drafting

1. **Recipient and relationship:** name, role and how you work together.
2. **Earlier events:** what you wrote or discussed, with known dates and original excerpts.
3. **Current request:** one action or question the recipient can answer.
4. **Tone:** for example, friendly, neutral and concise.
5. **Hard facts:** exact names, topic, options, actual commitments and constraints; list unknowns explicitly.
6. **Exclusions:** no invented agreement, deadline, delivery confirmation, motive for silence or new promise.

Keep the original source text under labels such as S1 and S2. ## Complete office example: ask a client to choose a room layout

All names, organizations, dates and messages in this example are fictional. The draft, audit and final are author-written and manually compared, not AI execution results. October 15 is the scenario date, not a rule about how long to wait.

### S1 — earlier email, October 8, 2026

```text
Date: October 8, 2026, 10:00 Europe/London
From: Maya Chen, project coordinator, Cedar Studio
To: Owen Patel, operations contact, Riverbend Learning Centre
Subject: Orientation room layout

Hi Owen,

Here are two room-layout options for the staff orientation at Riverbend
Learning Centre:

Room A: tables arranged in small groups.
Room B: chairs arranged in rows.

Could you let me know which option you prefer?

Thank you,
Maya Chen
```

### S2 — Maya's own note, October 15, 2026

```text
October 15, 2026, 09:00 Europe/London — Maya Chen
I checked this conversation for replies; none were visible to me.
I have no delivery or read confirmation. I have no recorded layout choice,
prior agreement, or promised reply date from Owen. My task is to ask which
room-layout option he prefers. I have no reply deadline to include.
```

S2 describes Maya's observation of that conversation. It does not establish what Owen received, read, decided or said elsewhere.

### Six inputs used in this example

Maya supplies Owen's client-contact role, the October 8 email and October 15 note, one request to choose Room A or Room B, a friendly neutral tone, the exact names/options/date, and exclusions for invented approval, deadlines, read status or promises. Agreement, room choice and a promised reply date remain unknown.

### A draft with errors to catch

```text
Subject: Re: Orientation room layout

Hi Owen,

I'm following up on the orientation room-layout options I emailed on October 8.
As agreed, we'll use Room B. Please confirm by October 16, the deadline you gave me.
Could you let me know whether you prefer Room A or Room B for the staff orientation?

Thank you,
Maya Chen
```

This draft invents an agreement and a deadline, then asks for a choice after asserting that one was made. “Please confirm” also competes with the actual request to choose. Do not send this version.

### Audit the assertions against the original sources

These are plain-English findings from a manual comparison, not model-generated status labels.

#### 1. Earlier email and date

**Claim:** I emailed orientation room-layout options on October 8.

**Original source and exact excerpt:** S1: “Date: October 8, 2026, 10:00 Europe/London”; “Here are two room-layout options for the staff orientation at Riverbend Learning Centre”.

**Gap / finding:** Supported by supplied fictional email; delivery/read status unknown.

**Human correction:** Keep only the sent-message reference.

**Approved final wording:** “I'm following up on the orientation room-layout options I emailed on October 8.”

#### 2. Prior agreement

**Claim:** There was a prior agreement (“As agreed”).

**Original source and exact excerpt:** No matching agreement in S1; S2: “I have no recorded layout choice, prior agreement, or promised reply date from Owen.”

**Gap / finding:** Unsupported; absence from this pack does not prove an agreement never existed elsewhere.

**Human correction:** Remove the agreement claim.

**Approved final wording:** Removed.

#### 3. Room B selection

**Claim:** We will use Room B.

**Original source and exact excerpt:** S1: “Room B: chairs arranged in rows.” S2: “I have no recorded layout choice”.

**Gap / finding:** An option is documented; its selection is unsupported.

**Human correction:** Remove the decision; ask for the choice.

**Approved final wording:** Removed; final question leaves both options open.

#### 4. Attributed reply deadline

**Claim:** Owen supplied an October 16 deadline.

**Original source and exact excerpt:** No matching excerpt. S2: “I have no reply deadline to include.”

**Gap / finding:** Unsupported attributed deadline. October 16 occurs only in the faulty draft.

**Human correction:** Delete the deadline and the additional confirmation request.

**Approved final wording:** Removed.

#### 5. Room options and event context

**Claim:** The options are Room A and Room B for the staff orientation (inside the question).

**Original source and exact excerpt:** S1: “for the staff orientation”; “Room A: tables arranged in small groups.”; “Room B: chairs arranged in rows.”

**Gap / finding:** Supported context embedded in a request; no selection implied.

**Human correction:** Keep the context.

**Approved final wording:** “Could you let me know whether you prefer Room A or Room B for the staff orientation?”

“Owen” and “Maya Chen” match S1's recipient/sender metadata. “I'm following up” states this message's purpose; “Thank you” is courtesy. The question is a request, not a factual claim in its own right, though its room options and event context can be checked. Do not force a SOURCED row for a pure request.

An audit label describes traceability to supplied text, **not truth**. Even a supported note could be mistaken. The prompt does not open links or verify what happened outside the source pack. Compare every assertion yourself and check tone separately.

## Short hiring follow-up example

Best,
Lena Ortiz

## Check before sending

- Can the recipient answer one clear question? Remove competing requests and unnecessary background.
- Do names, dates, options and any past promise match the original text? Preserve a proposed date as a proposal.
- Have you avoided inferring approval, rejection, receipt or intent from silence?
- Does the subject identify the topic, and are the actual recipients, signature and any intended attachment correct?
- Have you removed placeholders and private source notes, and reviewed what you share with any AI provider?

**When should I follow up?** Use the actual context: a promised reply date, real planning need, established working pattern and any relevant communication policy. There is no universal waiting period in this template. Do not turn the fictional interval into a schedule or repeated chase sequence.

**What if there is still no reply?** Reconsider whether the request is clear and the contact/channel is appropriate. Follow your organization's process where one exists; this page supplies wording, not an escalation policy or evidence about the recipient's intentions.
