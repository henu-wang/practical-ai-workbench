## Write a specific bullet from facts you can defend

A useful resume bullet explains what you did, where your responsibility started and ended, and any result you can actually support. Start with an action, add the task and relevant scope, then include an outcome only when you know it happened. A number is optional; an invented improvement is not a substitute for evidence.

## Download the evidence worksheet and examples

Use the editable [Word worksheet](../../templates/write-resume-bullet-points/resume-evidence-worksheet.docx) or [Markdown worksheet](../../templates/write-resume-bullet-points/resume-evidence-worksheet.md). The [four before-and-after examples](../../templates/write-resume-bullet-points/before-after-examples.md) contain entirely fictional work histories. They show how to revise a bullet, not achievements to copy into your resume.

For each bullet, write down your actual action, audience, tools, frequency, and result. Keep a separate box for facts you do not know. A team result does not automatically establish your individual contribution. Record what you personally handled before choosing “led,” “owned,” or “supported.”

## Make the wording concrete without making up impact

These examples use only their accompanying fact notes:

| Situation | Weak or unsupported draft | Checked rewrite |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volunteer administration | Handled scheduling and stuff. | Coordinated a shared calendar for six tutoring volunteers, sent session reminders, and checked bookings twice a week. |
| Customer support, qualitative result | Helped with customer emails. | Drafted an FAQ reply guide adopted by the team after supervisor review, and escalated customer questions outside its answers. |
| Inventory work, outcome unknown | Improved inventory accuracy by 40%. | Checked studio equipment against the inventory list, logged missing labels, and reported discrepancies to the coordinator. |
| Student research contribution | Led a major research breakthrough. | Organized interview notes by theme and prepared summaries for an advisor's review. |

The first example has a defensible scope count, not a claimed performance gain. The second has a verified qualitative result—the team adopted the guide—without inventing a response-time or satisfaction metric. The third removes an unsupported percentage. The fourth replaces inflated leadership with the actual contribution. Each rewrite is under 30 words using a whitespace word count.

If you have no measured outcome, describe the task and scope clearly. If you remember that a process improved but cannot establish the amount, do not turn that memory into a percentage. Leave an explicit question in the worksheet and seek an appropriate source.

**Should every bullet have a number?** No. Use a verified quantity when it clarifies your work; otherwise use specific actions and scope.

**Will these bullets pass an ATS or secure an interview?** This recipe makes no such guarantee. Wording and formatting checks cannot establish an employer's screening decision.
