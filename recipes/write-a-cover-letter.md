## Write a cover letter that connects the job to your experience

Start with the actual job posting. Pick two or three important requirements, match each to something you have done, and explain those connections in short paragraphs. Open with the role and your strongest relevant experience; close with a simple invitation to discuss it. Follow the employer's instructions on length and format, and leave unsupported skills out.

## Decide what belongs in each paragraph

The opening answers “Which job, and why this candidate?” Use the exact role title and employer name. Avoid a claim about the company's culture unless you have an actual source for it.

The middle explains relevant actions, rather than listing adjectives. A sentence such as “I am exceptionally organized” gives the reader little to evaluate. “I maintained a shared booking sheet and sent session reminders” tells them what you handled. Experience can come from paid work, volunteering or a project; describe its setting accurately.

The closing can express interest and invite a conversation. Do not predict selection or imply that a skill gap has already been resolved. A cover letter complements the resume by explaining fit; it need not retell every role.

## See a requirement turn into a supported claim

Our fictional Northbank Community Centre seeks a **Front Desk Assistant** to answer visitor questions, maintain room bookings and hand over unresolved requests. Its posting prefers RoomBook experience and asks for a cover letter of no more than 500 words.

For this example, the applicant requests a short draft of about 200 words. That explicit preference overrides the primary prompt's default 250–350 words while staying within the employer's 500-word maximum. The complete example below is 198 words by whitespace count, including its greeting and sign-off.

| Posting requirement | Fictional applicant's evidence | Wording decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Answer visitor questions | E1: explained a tutoring programme's published timetable and sign-up process | Describe that setting; do not invent a paid reception job |
| Maintain bookings | E2: updated a shared Google Sheets booking list and sent reminders | Name the actual tool; do not replace it with RoomBook |
| Hand over unresolved requests | E3: recorded questions outside the programme's published guidance for the coordinator | Explain the boundary of responsibility |
| RoomBook preferred | E4: never used RoomBook; willing to learn it | State the gap rather than claiming proficiency |

The complete letter reads:

> Dear Hiring Team,
>
> I am applying for the Front Desk Assistant role at Northbank Community Centre. Through volunteering with a weekend tutoring programme, I have experience answering visitor questions, keeping booking information current and passing unresolved requests to a coordinator. Your posting describes these as part of the role, and they are the responsibilities I would like to build on.
>
> At the tutoring programme, I explained the published timetable and sign-up process to visitors. When a question fell outside the published guidance, I recorded it for the coordinator instead of giving an answer I could not confirm. This experience is relevant to your request for clear visitor information and careful handovers.
>
> I also updated a shared Google Sheets booking list and sent session reminders. My responsibility was to keep that list current, not to manage a commercial booking system. I have not used RoomBook, which your posting lists as preferred, and would be willing to learn it. I can offer the booking-list experience described above without claiming familiarity with software I have not used.
>
> I would welcome the opportunity to discuss how this experience could support your front desk. Thank you for considering my application.
>
> Sincerely,
> Alex Morgan

The map supports its claims, but does not establish professional reception experience or performance improvements. Those remain absent. For your letter, replace the entire example with your own facts.

**What if I lack a requested skill?** Keep the gap visible. Adjacent experience or willingness to learn is different from proficiency, and an essential requirement may still make the role unsuitable.

**Should I use the same letter for every job?** Reuse your verified facts and template, then remap them to the new posting.
