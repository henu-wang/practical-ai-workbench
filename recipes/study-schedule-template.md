## Build your week from available hours, then assign the work

Use a weekly study schedule to connect each task to a real time slot and a result you can check. First subtract work, classes, travel, meals and other fixed commitments. Then assign reading, recall, practice and review within the remaining time, keeping a buffer. If the work does not fit, show the gap rather than filling evenings you do not have.

## Fill the template in this order

Record the week and each fixed commitment first. Add usable slots as **day, start–end, minutes**. A two-hour gap is not two hours of study if travel or dinner occupies part of it. If availability varies, use dated slots rather than repeating the same week indefinitely.

Next list units, actual deadlines and your workload estimates. Mark an unknown date or estimate as unknown. A time estimate is a planning input you revise after working; it is not a fact about how long everyone needs.

Give each block an activity and an observable finish condition. For example, “answer five practice problems and mark the ones I cannot explain” is more useful than “study statistics.” Include recall or application as well as reading. Put the most time-sensitive work before its deadline, then check what remains uncovered.

The supplied unit estimates total **450 minutes**: information skills 120, academic writing 120, introductory statistics 120, and computing basics 90. Reserving 45 minutes leaves 390 for study, so **60 minutes of known work remain unplaced**. Another optional statistics reflection has an unknown date and duration and is listed separately; the 60-minute gap does not include it.

Monday allocates 45 minutes to reading information-skills notes and 15 to recall. Tuesday gives writing 30 minutes of reading and 30 of drafting. Wednesday gives statistics 30 minutes of reading and 15 of review; Thursday gives computing 30 of reading and 30 of practice. The downloads show every weekend block, deadline and completion check.

The example schedules 90 minutes each for information skills and writing, leaving 30 minutes uncovered in each. Statistics and computing receive their supplied estimates, which still does not prove mastery. Every dated task has some study before its deadline, but that is a minimum check, not complete coverage.

## Turn the scheduled recall block into an actual activity

Take Monday's **19:45–20:00 information-skills block**, its unit name and the eight supplied facts in the recall download. Pass these to the secondary TokRepo asset and request eight short-answer questions, with a single 15-minute activity. Compare each answer and reason with the supplied facts before using it.

The original worked activity uses eight minutes for answers with notes closed, five to check against the notes and two to record errors. One question asks what a claim is; its answer points to F1, the definition in the fictional notes. All eight questions include source references. No external fact is needed to answer them.

The secondary asset can also suggest a five-day plan. **Do not add that plan on top of this week's timetable.** Keep only supported questions and activities that fit the existing block. Any additional review must replace or use an explicitly available slot, and the totals must be recalculated. Do not sacrifice the buffer silently.

## Check the plan and handle missing time

Check start/end arithmetic, overlaps with obligations, deadlines, study totals plus buffer, and units without practice or review. In this example, 450 minutes cannot fit into 390. Choose a priority or discuss the workload; moving tasks between the same slots cannot create another hour. If you explicitly spend the buffer, only 45 of the 60 missing minutes fit, leaving 15 and no recovery margin. Unknown work remains unknown.

When you miss a block, record what was actually completed and replan the unfinished work. A missed Thursday block cannot be “caught up” on a fully occupied Saturday without displacing something else.
