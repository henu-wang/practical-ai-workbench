## Supply all five blocks to the writing prompt

Keep these five blocks separate and nonempty. Missing inputs need clarification before drafting.

1. **TASK:** the repeatable task and desired SOP output. Example: draft a procedure for preparing an orientation handout packet from an approved-file list.
2. **AUDIENCE AND RELATIONSHIP:** who follows it, who supplies inputs and who receives the result. Example: assistant prepares; coordinator receives the packet location and handles distribution.
3. **TONE:** plain, neutral action language suitable for a colleague.
4. **CONSTRAINTS:** use the blank's fields; mark DRAFT; include ordered steps, role, input, observable result, source and exceptions. Preserve unknowns. Invent no access, approval, folder-naming or retention rule.
5. **ROUGH DRAFT OR NOTES:** paste substantive original excerpts under stable labels such as N1–N5. Include supplied source locations, filenames and revisions where relevant. A list of labels or a model summary is insufficient.

Request the SOP body in the primary prompt's Draft section. Keep one complete draft for review, including its unknowns and exceptions. The primary prompt's own review is useful drafting assistance; it is not independent evidence.

## Worked example: an orientation handout packet

Everything below is synthetic, including the file list. The drafts and audit are author-written. No model was run and no files were copied or distributed.

### Original notes to supply

- **N1:** “The coordinator supplies the current approved-file list and a destination packet folder.”
- **N2:** “The assistant copies only listed files into the supplied folder without changing source files.”
- **N3:** “Before handoff, the assistant compares filenames and revision dates with the approved list.”
- **N4:** “If a listed file is missing or its revision differs, stop and ask the coordinator; do not substitute another version.”
- **N5:** “After checks, send the packet location to the coordinator, who handles distribution. Folder naming and retention policy have not been supplied.”

The downloadable example includes these originals, a fictional two-file list and the complete corrected SOP. Actual access, the named owner, approval authority and resume rules are not supplied; the example leaves them unresolved.

### Catch unsupported policy before accepting the draft

An intentionally faulty draft says: “A checked packet is automatically approved. Delete the packet after seven days.” Both sentences add policy absent from N1–N5.

The complement's introductory source advice conflicts with its missing/too-short-source stop rule: always provide substantive source text, or stop and obtain it. It does not open linked sources.

Here is the manual claim comparison for the example, presented as stacked records so it remains readable on a phone:

**Claim: a checked packet is automatically approved.** No original excerpt supports automatic approval. N5 says, “After checks, send the packet location to the coordinator, who handles distribution.” Distribution responsibility does not establish approval authority. Remove automatic approval; record owner decision as pending.

**Instruction with an unsupported policy implication: delete after seven days.** N5 says, “Folder naming and retention policy have not been supplied.” Remove the deletion instruction. Keep retention UNKNOWN and ask the process owner for the applicable rule. Even if an audit omits this imperative, the human step review must catch it.

**Claim: the coordinator handles distribution.** N5 explicitly says, “the coordinator, who handles distribution.” Keep this limited responsibility; do not add automatic sending by the assistant.

A claim grid checks traceability to supplied text, **not truth**. A source can be wrong. The audit cannot establish operational safety, permission, completeness or approval, and it does not rewrite the SOP. Check its findings yourself and make the edits.

### Corrected procedure draft

**Prepare orientation handout packet — DRAFT, owner approval pending.** Purpose: assemble the listed files for handoff to the coordinator. Trigger: receipt of the current approved-file list and destination folder. Scope: copying and checking; distribution belongs to the coordinator. Actual access must be confirmed before use.

1. **Obtain the list and destination.** Assistant uses the coordinator's supplied list and folder. Result: the exact list and destination are identified. Source: N1.
2. **Check the listed sources.** Assistant checks availability, filenames and revisions against that list before copying. Result: each listed file matches, or the process stops for clarification. Sources: N1, N3, N4. Checking before copying is the author's proposed sequence; N3 explicitly requires the final check before handoff.
3. **Copy only matching listed files.** Assistant copies into the supplied folder without changing originals. Result: copies of the listed files are present. Source: N2.
4. **Compare the packet with the list.** Assistant compares its filenames and revisions before handoff. Result: the list is accounted for, with no unlisted substitutes. Sources: N2, N3. Any missing or mismatched file invokes N4.
5. **Give the location to the coordinator.** Assistant provides the checked packet location; coordinator handles distribution. Result: location handed off, not an approval receipt. Source: N5.

**Exception:** stop and ask the coordinator for any missing file or revision mismatch; do not substitute. No automatic resume rule is supplied. **Open:** actual access, named process owner/reviewer, approval decision, folder naming, retention and resume rule. No deletion or automatic approval is authorized by these notes.

## Walk the actions separately, then obtain owner approval

Commands such as “copy” and “compare” may not appear as factual claims in a grid. Map **every action, role, input, result, sequence and exception** to a note yourself. Label additions as proposals for owner review. The downloaded source map does this for all five steps.

The author performed a **manual paper walkthrough**, using the fictional two-file list, without opening or copying files:

- **Matching case:** both filenames and revisions match the list; the paper sequence reaches copy, final comparison and location handoff. Neither approval nor distribution is marked complete.
- **Missing-file case:** the second file is absent at step 2; N4 blocks copying and handoff. The question goes to the coordinator; no substitute or invented restart is used.
- **Revision-mismatch case:** a different revision at step 2 blocks progress. A mismatch found in the final comparison also blocks handoff. N4 governs both; resolution and resume stay pending.

This checks the draft's written logic, not a real process or its permissions. Before operational use, have the actual process owner confirm source accuracy, proposed sequence, access, completion, exceptions and unresolved policy, and record their decision. Stop at unresolved inputs that prevent the task from being followed. A well-written draft is not an approved SOP.
